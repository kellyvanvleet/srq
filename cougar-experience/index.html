01 / Understand today. Design what comes next.

Start with the whole experience.

At DrFirst, I looked at the full customer and patient journey. I would bring that same product mindset here: understand what different families need and map their experience from first interest through their years at Mooney.

PART ONE / CURRENT STATE

Understand what happens today.

Listen to families and staff. Walk through inquiries, visits, applications, enrollment, and the first weeks of school. Review existing programs, tools, communication, and results. Where is the experience strong? Where do families need help?

What we create: a shared picture of the family journey, different family needs, and the opportunities that matter most.

PART TWO / FUTURE STATE

Propose what could work better.

Agree on priorities with leadership. Design clear next steps, relevant communication, helpful check-ins, and simple reporting. Build on what works and use technology where it makes the experience easier.

What we create: a practical proposal with responsibilities, measures, and changes the team can introduce gradually.

Mooney already offers tours, online applications, placement testing, and Cougar Experience. This concept explores how those connections could work together across the full family journey, guided by the school’s Catholic mission.

02 / The family journey

Every next step should feel clear.

What happens next? Who can help? Is anything missing? Select a step to see how family support, staff follow-through, and reporting could connect.

A connected journey, rather than a rigid sequence: tours may happen at different points; freshmen and transfer applicants have different requirements. School admissions policies remain the authority.

03 / Recruitment relationships

Deepen the roots. Broaden the reach.

Strengthen Catholic partner-school relationships and understand how other families discover Mooney. Match the outreach to the audience, the school’s mission, and available capacity.

04 / Make it sustainable

Personal attention. Reliable follow-through.

A great family experience needs a process the team can sustain. Start with the tools Mooney already uses and make the responsibilities clear.

Know the next step.

Keep a shared view of each applicant’s progress, what is missing, and who will help. Families should not have to repeat their story at every handoff.

Keep the promise.

Make new-family onboarding consistently clear and welcoming: the right information, useful introductions, a named contact, and a check-in after school begins.

Learn as a team.

Review follow-up regularly, listen to family feedback, and use each enrollment cycle to improve the next. Document the process and train the people who use it.

05 / Reporting that leads to action

See the experience. Know where to help.

Are families interested? Where are they getting stuck? Are new families settling in? Simple dashboards would help the team answer those questions and decide what to do next.

Illustrative dashboard · Made-up data
These examples show how reporting could work. They are not Mooney’s results, targets, or connected reports.

How the real reporting would stay reliable

Agree on definitions, reporting dates, and goals before measuring progress. Compare students entering the same school year, show counts with percentages, and separate freshmen from transfers. Track current school and how a family first heard about Mooney separately from later interactions.

Track outreach link clicks and event attendance alongside inquiries and applications. A click is an interaction, not necessarily a unique family. Enrollment yield means enrolled students divided by admitted students; retention means returning students divided by those eligible to return, excluding graduates. Keep access to individual family information limited to the staff who need it.

06 / Possibilities to explore

The right information. At the right time.

Families share a school community, but their needs are different. Technology could help Mooney provide relevant communication and timely support without requiring staff to write every message from scratch.

A helpful nudge.

Automation can send a reminder or invitation when it is useful, based on a family’s stage and preferences. Stop reminders when the task is complete.

A more relevant message.

AI could help staff prepare messages for different family needs and organize feedback into themes. Staff review the content and keep school information accurate.

A person when it matters.

Questions, concerns, and requests for help should reach a real person. A quiet inbox or missed event is a reason to ask, not assume what a family needs.

Begin with information families choose to share, such as interests, preferred language, communication preferences, or help they request with timing and transportation. Avoid inferring sensitive circumstances. Explore capabilities in existing systems before adding tools.

07 / Put the proposal into practice

Start small. Learn. Improve.

The pace and priorities would follow the school’s goals, calendar, resources, and what discovery reveals.

CHOOSE ONE PRIORITY

Agree on a useful first step.

For example, make follow-up after a campus visit more helpful. Understand the current experience, choose what to improve, and agree on what success would look like.

TRY IT IN PRACTICE

Make one improvement real.

Prepare the communication, assign responsibility, train the team, and try it with a manageable group. Keep the family’s next step clear.

MEASURE & REFINE

Use what we learn.

Review responses, progress, and family feedback. Did the change help? Adjust it, document it, and expand what works before taking on the next priority.
