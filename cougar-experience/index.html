01 / Start with what exists

Build on the connections already in place.

Mooney already has important pieces: campus tours, online applications, placement testing, and Cougar Experience, with opportunities for Catholic partner-school students and families from other school settings.

The opportunity I would explore: connect those touchpoints to a consistent family experience, with clear responsibilities, useful follow-up, and reporting that helps the team plan for the next enrollment cycle.

“Cougar Experience 2.0” is my proposed strategy name. It extends the idea beyond the existing campus event. I would begin by learning what already works and where the school wants to grow.

02 / The family journey

Every next step should feel clear.

Explore how the experience, the team’s responsibilities, and the measurement connect. These are proposed operating practices to validate with Mooney’s team.

A connected journey, rather than a rigid sequence: tours may happen at different points; freshmen and transfer applicants have different requirements. School admissions policies remain the authority.

03 / Recruitment relationships

Deepen the roots. Broaden the reach.

Strengthen Catholic partner-school relationships and understand how other families discover Mooney. Match the outreach to the audience, the school’s mission, and available capacity.

04 / Set it up for the future

A system the team can use, year after year.

The reporting only works if the underlying process is consistent. I would first review existing tools and workflows, then agree on a practical operating model with the people doing the work.

One shared view.

Consistent applicant records, entry year, grade, current school, recruitment source, stage, stage dates, owner, and next action. Distinguish households from individual applicants so counts stay accurate.

Clear handoffs.

Define what completes each stage, who owns follow-up, and what information moves to the next team. Agree on response expectations and exception handling with admissions, the registrar, counselors, and family-facing staff.

A useful rhythm.

Weekly pipeline and follow-up review. Monthly recruitment and family feedback review. An annual enrollment-cycle retrospective that informs next year’s calendar, outreach, and priorities.

Use existing systems where they work. Document definitions and responsibilities, train the team, and limit access to student and family information by role. Leadership reporting should use aggregate data.

05 / Measurement that supports decisions

Know what’s working. Know where to act.

Start with a reliable baseline, then agree on targets with leadership. Connect each measure to a decision, an owner, and a consistent review schedule.

Proposed reporting framework
MeasureConsistent definitionDecision it supports
Pipeline & follow-upApplicants by entry year, grade, stage, time in stage, and overdue next action.Where does a family need help? Who follows up?
Stage conversionApplicants reaching the next stage ÷ those entering the previous stage, using the same cohort and reporting window.Which transitions warrant closer review?
Enrollment yieldEnrolled students ÷ admitted students in the same entry cohort, measured at an agreed cutoff.What helps accepted families choose Mooney?
Recruitment contributionInquiries, applications, offers, and enrollments by current school and recruitment source. Keep first source separate from later touchpoints.Which relationships and activities merit investment?

RetentionEligible returning students who re-enroll ÷ students eligible to return. Exclude graduates and track withdrawals separately.What supports belonging and continued enrollment?

Review comparable points in each admissions cycle. Show counts alongside percentages, separate freshmen from transfers, and avoid overinterpreting small groups. Recruitment plans should also reflect seats, staffing, and school priorities.

06 / How I would begin

Learn. Connect. Improve.

An initial 90-day approach, shaped by the admissions calendar and leadership priorities.

DAYS 1–30 / DEFINE

Understand the whole picture.

Listen to staff, families, and partner-school leaders. Map the current journey, calendar, tools, responsibilities, and available data. Identify strengths and agree on the most useful baseline.

Output: a shared journey map and prioritized opportunities.

DAYS 31–60 / EXECUTE

Make one improvement real.

Choose a manageable pilot together, such as post-visit follow-up or a new-family handoff. Define ownership, prepare communications, train the people involved, and establish reporting.

Output: a working pilot with clear responsibilities.

DAYS 61–90 / EVALUATE & REFINE

Build on what we learn.

Review results and family feedback. Resolve friction, document the process, and recommend what to extend. Build a recruitment and engagement calendar that can improve each year.

Output: an evidence-informed plan for the next cycle.
